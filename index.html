AI-assisted analysis of Martian lava tubes

BS thesis, Institute of Space Technology | Supervisor: Dr Imran Ali Khan | Arsia Mons, Mars

Lava-tube skylights on Mars could shelter future explorers from radiation and extreme temperatures, but searching a huge archive of orbital images by eye is slow. I built a pipeline that detects candidate skylights with deep learning, checks them geologically, and ranks them for habitation.

15,114CTX image tiles scanned, cut from 999 georeferenced images
64% to 97%accuracy across three models: basic CNN, CNN with hard negatives, ResNet-50
about 2,600candidates left for manual review, roughly 17% of the tiles
146validated skylight candidates; 48 with complete data ranked
  • Georeferenced CTX images to a Mars coordinate system with GDAL, cut them into 256 x 256 pixel tiles, and labelled them automatically against a published skylight catalogue.
  • Compared three models. A basic CNN with random negatives reached 64.15% accuracy and one with hard negatives (pits, craters, collapse features) reached 71.70%. A three-class ResNet-50 (skylight, hard negative, image noise) reached 97.30% on validation data, trained on 328 augmented skylight tiles made from 41 confirmed ones.
  • Ran the final model on every tile, then checked detections against CTX, HiRISE, THEMIS and JMARS imagery to build the final inventory.
  • Designed a Colonization Suitability Index from four factors: thermal stability, subsurface protection, accessibility and atmospheric damping. Changing the weights left the top ten sites unchanged, and the top ten had deeper pits on average than the bottom ten (131.9 m against 53.8 m).

What I learned: only five AI detections passed every geological check, and all five were already in the published catalogue, so the model re-found known skylights and did not discover new ones. Training data was small and validation covered one volcanic province, so the method needs testing on other regions.

Tools: Python (PyTorch, OpenCV, NumPy, Pandas, scikit-learn, Matplotlib), GDAL, QGIS, ArcGIS Pro, JMARS. Data: NASA and USGS public datasets (CTX, HiRISE, THEMIS, MOLA).
